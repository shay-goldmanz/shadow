Epoch magazine's one-page spreads work because every spread commits to
exactly one idea and gives it the whole page instead of splitting the
page between three ideas each getting a third of the attention. A
one-pager for a decision should copy that discipline: one recommendation,
argued once, not three options weighed neutrally with the actual
recommendation buried in a "next steps" section nobody reaches.

## Structure

Open with the recommendation in the first sentence, not the last
paragraph. State the problem in one paragraph — what happens if nothing
changes. State the recommendation and the two or three reasons it wins,
each reason a single sentence a reader could quote back. Address the
strongest objection explicitly, in one paragraph, rather than hoping the
reader doesn't think of it. End with what happens next if the reader
agrees, in one line.

## What to leave out

Leave out alternatives that were considered and clearly rejected —
mention that alternatives were considered, in one sentence, without
re-litigating each one. A one-pager that spends half its length
defending against options it already ruled out is not confident in its
own recommendation. Leave out background the target reader already
has; a one-pager written for a team that lived through the problem does
not need to re-explain the problem to them.

## Density is a form of respect

Every sentence should survive being read exactly once, at reading
speed, by someone who will not scroll back up. That standard is what
"editorial density" means in practice: not more words per inch, but
zero words that do not need to be there.
